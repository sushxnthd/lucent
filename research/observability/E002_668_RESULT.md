# E002-668 | Finite exposure defeats point-phase coverage (2026-11-06 UTC)

## New falsification
E002-667's six-phase geometric covering guarantee establishes that a commanded **instant** can fall in a closed interval of a phase-stationary periodic fixture. It does **not** guarantee that a finite-exposure camera will classify that observation as closed. Freeze a rectangular integration kernel of width w=0.60 cycles, a periodic completely closed interval of width c=0.30, open aperture 1 otherwise, and a detector that declares closure only if its exposure-averaged aperture is <=tau=0.30. Even a perfectly centered exposure over the closure averages 0.50, so **none** of six grid commands can register closure at this threshold, despite geometric phase coverage. This is a synthetic, deliberately adverse fixture, not a measured human blink.

## Dimensionless bound and proof
For any integration window of width w and a single closed interval of width c, the closed exposure fraction is at most min(1,c/w). The average aperture is therefore at least max(0,1-c/w). Detection at threshold tau requires overlap fraction >=1-tau. In particular, if w>c/(1-tau), **no phase** can trigger a threshold-based closure detection. Here c/(1-tau)=3/7<3/5=w, hence even an ideal phase oracle fails. The claim assumes binary aperture, rectangular nonnegative normalized shutter response, exact periodicity, and a single contiguous closure interval per period. For arbitrary calibrated kernels the appropriate criterion is maximal integrated closed-phase kernel mass, not c/w. Brightness, gaze and geometry could further invalidate aperture recovery.

## Frozen physical validation
On an independently timed LED/mechanical shutter with known duty cycle, collect paired long- and short-exposure images at the same six phases, measure exposure kernels and linearity separately with a photodiode and unsaturated reference, and preregister threshold tau. Predict zero detected closures at w=0.60,c=0.30,tau=0.30, but detection at the center phase for w=0.05. Include randomized phase order, ±0.05-cycle timing perturbations, illumination drift, blur, rolling-shutter row offsets, missed frames, and detector saturation. A long-exposure positive detection at the frozen threshold falsifies the stipulated binary/kernel model or its calibration. A short-exposure miss at the center falsifies its positive control.
