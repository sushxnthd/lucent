# E002-729: high-rate human evidence candidate and frozen admissibility gate (2026-11-08 UTC)

## New primary-source lead
Garbin et al., *OpenEDS: Open Eye Dataset* (2019), https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03702, describe **200 Hz synchronized eye-facing cameras**, **152 participants**, and **91,200 video-sequence frames** (1.5-second clips). Source says access **upon request** at https://research.fb.com/programs/openeds-challenge . This is a candidate high-rate human observation source, **not yet acquired, inspected, or validated**. Eye-region segmentation annotations alone do not prove eyelid-aperture or complete-closure labels; the video sequences require verification of format, timestamp accuracy, eyelid visibility and participant IDs before inclusion.

Contrast: mEBAL2 (https://github.com/BiDAlab/mEBAL2) provides 180 subjects and blink events, but the dataset requires a signed license and supplied RGB/NIR videos are not documented as high-rate ground truth; it cannot independently certify subframe closure from 30 Hz video. MPEblink (https://zenodo.org/records/7754768) is in-the-wild blink detection, not a known high-speed reference. **Do not treat blink annotations or nominal FPS as ground-truth physical exposure intervals.**

## Predeclared held-out experiment, conditional on acquiring OpenEDS
1. Verify acquisition frame rate and timing from original metadata; separate actual exposure timing from frame presentation time. Reject clips with unknown clock/exposure timing for physical certificates; allow explicitly labeled exploratory tests only.
2. Fix participants into disjoint calibration and held-out partitions *before* fitting any velocity ceiling, aperture normalization, detector or nuisance thresholds. No cross-eye or same-subject leakage. Log participant IDs and SHA-256 manifests.
3. Calibrate eye-aperture intervals (including occlusion/landmark uncertainty) and a conservative velocity upper bound from calibration participants only; test how much the ceiling changes with gaze, geometry, illumination, glasses, blur, missingness and timing perturbations. If a justified bound cannot be established, all exclusion decisions abstain.
4. Freeze camera exposure schedules and decimation rates (15/24/30/60 Hz); reconstruct ordinary-camera observations from high-rate frames only as a **sampling proxy**, never as physical long-exposure emulation. Any claims involving rolling shutter or exposure integration require a separately calibrated instrument.
5. Predict exclusion of a hidden full closure only when the *strict*, uncertainty-aware E002-371 inequality holds for every admissible aperture/velocity/timing realization. Exact equality abstains. Compare held-out high-rate traces for observed intervening closures, with transition intervals and unresolved labels explicitly retained.
6. Primary safety endpoint: **false exclusions among issued exclusion certificates**, stratified by participant and nuisance; also report certificate coverage, abstentions, ambiguous reference intervals, and participant-clustered uncertainty. A 0-error synthetic result is not human validation.
7. Stop or retract the corresponding product observable on any confidently established false exclusion under the declared assumptions. No fatigue, sleepiness, sleep-stage, or alarm-optimization claims follow from blink observability alone.
