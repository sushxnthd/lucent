# Sharp conditional identification (not novel mathematics)
The physical RGB image formation depends on pupil/iris anatomical ratio q(t), differential optical magnification m(g,t), eyelid aperture a(t), brightness I(t), point-spread function, shutter intervals and ISP. A measured pupil/iris feature is NOT generally the shutter integral of anatomical radius. A local surrogate y_j=alpha*h_j+sum_k N_jk*beta_k+e_j is valid only after an independent calibration residual bound.
Let F={(alpha,beta): -eta<=y-alpha*h-N*beta<=eta, l<=beta<=u}. If nonempty, its projection on alpha is a closed polyhedral interval, ray, or R. Its sharp endpoints are the LP minimum and maximum of alpha over F. Zero excluded iff a sign is certified. Each attained endpoint has an explicit feasible witness. Proof: F is a closed polyhedron; linear projections of polyhedra are polyhedra; optimize the coordinate alpha.
Exact two-frame example: y=(3/50,3/50), h=(1,1), nuisance n_g=(1,1), n_b=(1,-1), |beta_g|<=B, |beta_b|<=1/50, |e_i|<=1/100. Then alpha in [1/20-B,7/100+B]. Lower witness beta_g=B, beta_b=0, e=(1/100,1/100); upper witness beta_g=-B, beta_b=0, e=(-1/100,-1/100). Bcrit=1/20. With unbounded beta_g, gauge (alpha,beta_g)->(alpha+t,beta_g-t) yields identical observations for every t.
The impossibility is for the calibrated feature/surrogate, not necessarily complete RGB pixels. Human physics may violate N and eta; that is a falsification, not evidence for the certificate.
