# E002-324 exact clock-gauge interval
### Explicit upstream RGB measurement equation
For RGB channel c and frame n, the digital image is modeled as
I[c,n](u) = Q[c,n]{ integral over exposure W[n](tau) * [ K[c,n] * F[c](u; pupil_radius(tau), iris_radius(tau), eyelid_aperture(tau), gaze(tau), illumination(tau), glasses(tau), scale(tau)) ] d tau } + pixel_noise[c,n](u).
Q is an unknown or calibrated camera response, K is optical blur, and W is the exposure weighting. A normalized ocular feature f_RGB[n] exists only if its spatial and photometric nuisance gates have passed. The high-rate reference is f_ref[k] = f_anatomical(t_ref[k]-ell)+reference_error[k], subject to a frozen signal-pair mapping.

### Timing model
At event time t, a perfect feature-based lag estimator on the two nominal clocks reports y(t)=ell(t)+b(t)+e(t), where b(t) is the camera-minus-reference clock offset, including timestamp conventions, and e(t) aggregates lag estimation error, exposure-midpoint error, and residual signal mapping. Assume |e(t)|<=eta established independently, not estimated from holdout outcomes.

### Theorem
If b(t) is affine on [a,z] and calibrated anchor intervals are |b(a)-ma|<=da and |b(z)-mz|<=dz, then define wa=(z-t)/(z-a), wz=(t-a)/(z-a). The sharp identified interval is
ell(t) in [y(t)-m-R, y(t)-m+R],
m=wa*ma+wz*mz, R=|wa| da+|wz| dz+eta.
This remains algebraically valid for extrapolation t outside [a,z] **only while the affine drift assumption remains valid there**.

Proof: affine interpolation gives b(t)=wa b(a)+wz b(z). Independent endpoint intervals form a rectangle; a linear functional on a rectangle attains its extrema at corners, giving b(t) in [m-(|wa|da+|wz|dz),m+(|wa|da+|wz|dz)]. Independent bounded e adds [-eta,+eta]. Every endpoint is attained by choosing anchor signs according to wa,wz and e sign. Thus interval is both necessary and sufficient. The sign criterion follows immediately.

### Exact impossibility witnesses
Observed y=5 ms can be generated by (ell=+5 ms,b=0) or (ell=-5 ms,b=+10 ms), producing the same apparent alignment. Without an independent bound on b, even the SIGN of physiological delay is unidentifiable. With only one clock anchor and unrestricted affine slope, b(t)=b(a)+s(t-a), so any ell is feasible at t!=a.

### Scope limits
This is conditional timing identifiability, not a proof that RGB features are anatomical, that a lag is physiologically causal, or that an affine clock holds over long sessions. A camera exposure integral can itself destroy the waveform, and low-pass preprocessing (e.g., 10-Hz loaders) can destroy high-frequency lag information. Do not apply the theorem if the upstream feature or estimator error bound is not independently justified.
