# E002-330 exact model-conditional observability laws

**Full camera model.** A useful general forward equation is Y[j,c,u]=Q_c{ E_j^(-1) ∫_[t_j,t_j+E_j] K_(j,t)*[G_(j,c)(t,u) M(t,u) F_c(u;r(t),R,H(t),Z_p(t),Z_i(t),reflectance(t))+B_(j,c)(t,u)] dt + noise_(j,c,u)}. Here r is physiological pupil radius, R iris radius, H gaze/head projection, Z effective optical depth/refraction, M eyelid/glasses aperture, G/B photometry/ISP, K blur, Q quantization, and native exposure and clock matter. Extracting a contour from an integrated image does NOT generally commute with integration.

**A: exact full-image temporal permutation gauge.** With a memoryless renderer F(u;r), fixed scene geometry/photometry and uniform global shutter, I(u)=1/T ∫F(u;r(t))dt=∫F(u;x)dμ_r(x). The RGB frame depends only on the occupancy distribution μ_r of radii, not their time order. Every measure-preserving rearrangement produces identical full RGB images at every pixel and color. In particular r_A(t)=r0+a sin(2πt/T), r_B(t)=r0-a sin(2πt/T) give identical exposure images but opposite trajectories and radii r0±a at T/4. This is an exact impossibility for within-exposure phase, even with infinite pixel resolution and zero noise. It is not an impossibility claim for independently calibrated rolling-shutter row windows, coded shutters or strong additional dynamics assumptions.

**B: positive idealized occupancy invariant.** If pupil and iris are fully visible concentric disks with fixed center and linear calibrated intensities C_p != C_i, the radial pixel intensity is I(ρ)=C_i+(C_p-C_i)Pr[r(t)≥ρ]. Thus the radial intensity profile recovers the radius survival/occupancy function, NOT temporal order. Moments: E[r^k]=k∫ρ^(k-1)S(ρ)dρ. With bounded intensity error η and contrast D=|C_p-C_i|, occupancy error is ≤η/D. Two occupancy hypotheses separated by ΔS are guaranteed distinguishable iff D|ΔS|>2η. Equality is ambiguous.

**C: sharp common-visibility bound.** For a separately calibrated scalar pupil-area channel q(t)=r(t)^2/R^2 in [0,1], suppose y=∫wq/∫w with 0<w_min≤w≤w_max and κ=w_max/w_min. The true unweighted mean f=E[q] obeys the SHARP interval
    y/[κ(1-y)+y] ≤ f ≤ κy/[1-y+κy].
Proof: E[wq]/E[w(1-q)] lies between f/[κ(1-f)] and κf/(1-f); invert. Both endpoints are attained by binary q and weights taking the two extremes. Maximum |y-f| is exactly (sqrt(κ)-1)/(sqrt(κ)+1), attained with binary q and high weights where q=1 at fraction 1/(1+sqrt κ). With observed yhat±δ, substitute ylo=max(0,yhat-δ) and yhi=min(1,yhat+δ) into lower and upper bounds. Certify f>θ only if κ<ylo(1-θ)/[θ(1-ylo)] (interior case); equality abstains.

This common-weight law DOES NOT hold if pupil/iris visibility differs, nonlinear gamma is unknown, or contour fitting has unmodeled bias. If w_min=0 because a blink can hide the pupil, no nontrivial bound on the entire exposure mean follows without assumptions. With known unobserved fraction m and uniform visible weighting, f lies sharply in [(1-m)y,(1-m)y+m].

**Product.** These are conditional measurement facts, not real-webcam validation. Reject rapid pupil phase/lag and five-second fatigue diagnosis; allow only calibrated, abstaining occupancy research after human reference validation. Geometry, lighting, gaze, glasses, cornea and timebase remain separate failure gates.
